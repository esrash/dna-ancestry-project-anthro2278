Understanding DNA ancestry

ANTHRO 2278A-650 · Enhanced Contribution

How a sample becomes an estimate

An ancestry result is an inference based on comparisons. It is not a direct reading of race or culture from DNA.

01

Look at genetic variation

Testing examines selected genetic markers in a person's DNA. Different tests follow different patterns of inheritance.

02

Compare reference samples

Patterns are compared with samples from people used to represent particular populations. Who is included in those samples matters.

03

Infer an ancestry estimate

Statistical methods estimate relationships to the reference groups. Labels, samples, and methods shape the result and its uncertainty.

Original explanatory diagram. Conceptual source: Royal et al. (2010). This explains principles, not any company's current testing system.

Different tests, different windows

Autosomal DNA

Examines inherited variation across many family lines, rather than one direct maternal or paternal line.

Mitochondrial DNA

Follows a direct maternal line. That line is only one path through a much larger family tree.

Y-chromosome DNA

Follows a direct paternal line in people with a Y chromosome. It does not represent the whole family tree.

Source: Royal et al. (2010).

Three meanings of ancestry

A family connection, inherited DNA, and similarity to a reference sample describe different things. (Mathieson & Scally, 2020)

Genealogical ancestry

Your genealogical ancestors are the people in your family pedigree. A place in that pedigree does not guarantee that a distant ancestor contributed an identifiable segment of DNA to you.
